# Trading knowledge

The owner delegates trading judgment to Claude ([DECISIONS](DECISIONS.md#2026-11-02--claude-is-the-projects-systematic-trader-the-trading-knowledge)). This document is the knowledge Claude holds in that role: what the evidence says, how the app measures itself, what the record shows, and how to judge a change. Every thread that plans, judges or builds anything touching the trader, its prompt, model or provider, the policy, sizing, stops, exits, costs, the twins, the scores or a trading idea reads it first and applies [the review](#how-to-judge-a-trading-change) (the `trading-review` skill in `.claude/skills/`).

It grants nothing. [AUTONOMY_MATRIX](AUTONOMY_MATRIX.md) owns permissions, [RISK_POLICY](RISK_POLICY.md) owns every limit, and rules 1 to 5 of [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) stand over it. Sources are cited as `TR-nn` from the [trading research register](REFERENCES.md#trading-research--2026-11-01-and-2026-11-02) and `TB-nn` from the [builder and LLM-trading register](REFERENCES.md#trading-bot-builder-and-llm-trading-research--2026-10-17); the app's own numbers carry their label and date.

## The role: a systematic trader, not a discretionary one

Claude works as a **systematic trader**: part quant researcher, part risk manager. Not the discretionary "professional trader" who reads the last candles and calls the next move, because that is the trader who loses as a group: 97% of the persistent Brazilian index-futures day traders lost money with no sign of learning (TR-15), fewer than 1% of about 360,000 Taiwanese day traders a year were predictably profitable (TR-16), and 74 to 89% of retail CFD accounts lose (TR-17). Systematic macro funds did at least as well as discretionary ones after risk adjustment over 1996 to 2014 (TR-11). The knowledge that survives in trading is what is measured.

What the role means in every thread:

- **Capital first.** Protection in code, sized by volatility, latched on losses; a change that weakens any of it is refused before it is discussed.
- **Costs before signal.** A trade whose expected move cannot pay its fees, slippage and funding is not a trade, whatever the call.
- **Evidence over stories.** A claim about the trader is a paired measurement against a free rule on the same hours, with an interval, or it is "too few". A reason that sounds right is a hypothesis.
- **One change at a time, written down first.** The measure, the opponent, the sample and the pass rule go in the plan before the first reading; every variant tried counts against the winner.
- **Plain words to the owner.** What changes, what it costs (calls, tokens, time), what it resets, the recommendation and why; the owner picks on a card when the choice is theirs.

## The principles, with the evidence

1. **Costs set the pace.** Over 30 days of Binance 1-minute data the median 15-minute BTC move was 0.074%, below the 0.10% a round trip of taker fees costs; 38% of 15-minute bars moved more than that, against 64% of hours ([0039 §15](archive/plans/0039-bitcoin-futures-on-paper.md#15-progress)). The predictable return at quarter-hour marks on Binance perpetuals is about 0.5 bp gross, a tenth of a taker fee (TR-05); machine learning on 1- to 60-minute Bitcoin moves turned negative at 0.30% round-trip costs (TR-07). Here: no trade whose expected move cannot pay fees, slippage and funding; never trade faster; a cost gate before an entry is the one change the data already supports.
2. **Size by volatility, every trade the same.** 0.25% of equity at an ATR stop is volatility targeting at the trade level. Across 60+ assets since 1927 volatility scaling cut extreme returns and drawdowns and raised the Sharpe ratio of risk assets (TR-10); trend funds size every market to one volatility (TR-03). Here: the risk fraction and the ATR stop stay; a label, a streak or a model's confidence never sizes a position.
3. **Code owns exits, leverage and latches.** Systematic macro funds returned 5.01% a year against 2.86% for discretionary ones, and at least as well after adjustment (TR-11); 2x is the cap ESMA set for retail crypto CFDs (TR-17). Here: a stop is never loosened, a hold never extended, a limit never moved from a prompt or a page.
4. **The signal's horizon must match the hold, and the evidence is at slow horizons.** Past returns predict the next 1 to 12 months in 58 futures markets (TR-01); for Bitcoin this week's return predicts the next 1 to 4 weeks while the one-day effect is not robust (TR-02); the best Bitcoin moving-average windows were 141 and 781 hours, in sample and before costs (TR-04). At high frequency the autocorrelation decays rapidly (TR-06); the best 15-minute model reaches 57% for 0.5 bp (TR-05); 1- to 60-minute machine learning reached 50.9 to 56.0% (TR-07). The trader's 1-hour calls hold nothing past the hour: 46% at 4 hours ([the record](#the-record-so-far)). Here: a 1-hour call attached to a 4- to 24-hour hold is the design's known weakness; prefer slower horizons, and score a call at the horizon of the trade it opens.
5. **A language model reads text; it does not forecast numbers.** Removing the language model from LLM-based time-series forecasters does not degrade them (TR-19); where models show signal is headlines (TR-20). The trader agrees with the last-hour rule 83% of the time, and its deviations are a coin flip (22 right, 23 wrong); it cited news in 5 of 405 calls (TR-36). Here: put the model where it reads context (a veto, a release, a headline) and the direction rule in code; a prompt change cannot add information the features lack.
6. **A stated confidence is not a probability.** Models stating their confidence tend to be overconfident (TR-26); here `MEDIUM` calls were right 58.7% of the time and `LOW` 57.6%, `MEDIUM` hours simply moved more (0.44% against 0.28%), and a retry of an identical context changed the label (TR-36). Here: an entry switch on a self-report is a flaw to replace with a probability calibrated in code; until then, read the label as volatility, not accuracy.
7. **Funding is a priced cost.** Perpetual funding is positive about 85% of the time and made 7.39 of 17.89 percentage points of a BTC basis strategy's return (TR-13). Here: a funding filter belongs in code (the gated twin has one; the trader has advice only).
8. **Stops help under momentum and hurt under reversion.** Stop rules add value when returns persist and subtract it when they revert; short-horizon rules had negative stopping premiums (TR-12). Here: a 2 ATR stop on 15-minute candles mostly realises noise and pays fees; the stop multiple is a tested parameter, not a safety that comes free.
9. **One market is no diversification.** Trend funds earned a net Sharpe of 0.77 over 134 years by holding 67 markets (TR-03); on BTC alone moving-average rules were inconsistent walk-forward (TR-04). Here: expect no fund-like results from one perpetual, and never infer an edge from one market's good week.
10. **Measure, or it did not happen.** A hit rate over 30 calls carries ±18 points of noise, a day's 60 calls ±13, a week's 400 ±5; proving 55% needs about 617 independent decisive calls and 53% about 1,700, more with overlap; a Sharpe of 1 needs about 4 years to reach t = 2 (TR-35). The test is paired against the free rule on the same hours, scored by the mean move in the called direction, with a block bootstrap for overlap, a confidence sequence for daily reading (TR-33), the Pesaran–Timmermann test for direction (TR-34), and a deflated Sharpe with a trial ledger when picking among versions (TR-29, TR-30, TR-31, TR-32). Here: "too few" is the honest label until the interval clears zero; a coin flip is never the opponent.
11. **Loops overfit; one champion at a time.** Self-correction works only with reliable external feedback (TR-24, TR-25), and hourly outcomes are mostly noise; published language-model traders mostly trail buy-and-hold (TB-12, TB-13, TR-22, TR-23, TR-18); a model tested on dates before its cutoff may recall the outcome (TB-01, TR-21). Here: the trader stays frozen between installs, challengers run as twins or offline replays, promotion is an update the owner installs after a pre-registered pass, and every generation tried counts against the winner.
12. **The three camps that profit do not guess the next hour.** Trend-followers hold a long or a short in every market on 1- to 12-month signals (TR-03); market makers earn the spread and treat direction as a random walk (TR-14); crypto bots that document money earn carry (TR-13). The app can run none of them as built (one market, taker fills, no spot leg), and its trader resembles the camp that loses. Here: the risk rules make that trader lose slowly, not win; the role's job is to move the design toward rules in code with the model as a reader, and to say so plainly.

## How to judge a trading change

The `trading-review` skill holds the procedure; this is its short form. Answer each point with its label (`OBSERVED`, `VERIFIED`, `CALCULATED`, `NOT RUN`, opinion).

1. **Rules first.** Paper only, code's authority, no loosened protection, no moved limit, no research the owner has not switched on ([ExecPlan 0035](archive/plans/0035-a-simpler-app-that-trades-by-itself.md)). If it fails here, stop.
2. **Classify it.** Does the rule, its policy or its cost decide or fill differently? Then it is a new variant of the slow-rule family, counted in the trial ledger, and restarts M1. A page, the backtest's display or a read-only query does not.
3. **Costs.** The expected move against fees, slippage and funding at the horizon it trades.
4. **Horizon.** Does the signal's horizon match the hold, and is there evidence at that horizon (principle 4)?
5. **Where a model would sit.** None runs since [ExecPlan 0060](plans/0060-spot-trading-only.md); bringing one back is a new plan the owner approves. Reading text or saying no is plausible; forecasting numbers needs the paired test first.
6. **The yardstick.** Which opponent, paired on what, with what sample from principle 10; "too few" until then.
7. **Pre-registration.** Measure, opponent, sample and pass rule in the ExecPlan before the first reading; the trial ledger counts it.
8. **The clock.** Say whether it restarts a running measure (from 0060's install the spot rule's M1 to M3 run, and nothing else), and what it costs in days.

The answer to the owner is at most eight lines of plain words: what changes, what it costs, what it resets, the recommendation and why. A choice that is the owner's goes on a decision card with the recommended option marked.

## What not to propose

- Trading faster: fees are fixed per trade while moves shrink with the square root of time (principle 1).
- The model reading candles for alpha, a bigger model, more reasoning or a tuned prompt as a source of edge (principles 5 and 11); a provider switch as a quick try, since it changes the model under the running test.
- Several models voting: noise reduction, not alpha.
- Backtesting the language model on dates before its cutoff (TB-01, TR-21).
- News on one coin as a signal before an event study holds out of sample (the sentiment results came from choosing among thousands of stocks).
- A rule that trades the hit rate's recent streak, or a scorecard window too small to mean anything (principle 10).
- Shorting, borrowing or leverage in any form (rule 1, since [ExecPlan 0060](plans/0060-spot-trading-only.md)).
- Loosening a stop, extending a hold, raising a limit, a carry or basis twin (no futures leg since 0060), or anything on [ROADMAP's later list](ROADMAP.md#later-and-separately-approved).
