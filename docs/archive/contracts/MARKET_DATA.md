## Implemented M2 observation boundary

The optional market worker collects a fixed two-symbol universe from unauthenticated public endpoints. It resolves the fixed hosts, rejects any nonpublic DNS result and connects to the checked literal address while retaining TLS host verification. It rejects redirects, private request parameters, oversized responses and unapproved paths. REST requests stay under the worker's own weight ceiling, under a guard on the IP-wide weight the provider reports, and behind any provider back-off. The stream uses one combined subscription for each currently validated symbol's `@ticker` and `@kline_1h` channels. It has no browser-facing exchange connection, depth book or trade tape.

An instrument is display-eligible only with `TRADING`, `isSpotTradingAllowed=true`, required price/lot filters and a SPOT permission path. Binance's current `permissionSets` has OR alternatives within each set and AND across sets; an empty legacy `permissions` array does not itself deny SPOT. Unknown filters remain preserved and are not silently implemented as paper-order rules. Quotes are timestamped sampled best bid/ask with displayed quantities only. They are never executable prices or a guarantee of available liquidity.

The worker retrieves up to 720 prior UTC 1h rows per symbol. REST rows become finalized only when the conservative provider-clock lower bound is beyond their close; stream rows remain provisional until the provider marks them closed. All raw payloads and exact decimal observations are stored before a separate fenced publication transaction assigns application availability and a sequence. Browser/API reads see only published data and support a committed watermark. Backfilled bars retain the actual retrieval availability; they do not fill a forward operating gap.

The worker uses a separate 10-second market lease with two-second renewal. A lost lease blocks publication. Clock, instrument and quote age, message silence, worker lease, gap/incident counts and storage pressure appear separately in health. A connected socket alone cannot make a quote eligible. The worker defaults disabled after an M1 upgrade, and no M2 state can enable orders or fills.

The completion implementation gives each connection generation an immutable collection UUID and advances the fenced epoch before revalidation. Each frame is bound at receipt to its generation and validated instrument snapshot, and its captures keep that binding. A quote is usable only when its generation and snapshot equal the feed's pointers. A reconnect after a failure resets both pointers, so usability returns only with a quote captured by the new generation. Publication recovery never changes capture identity; unavailable legacy metadata stays null. One writer publishes deduplicated observations.

[ExecPlan 0012](../plans/0012-market-stream-continuity.md) keeps quotes continuous across instrument changes and rotations without weakening that rule:

- **Changed snapshot, unchanged subscription.** Binance recomputes `MARKET_LOT_SIZE.maxQty` roughly hourly; every other field was observed unchanged. The worker adopts the new snapshot on the live socket. That symbol's snapshot pointer and check time move in the same fenced publication transaction as its first quote bound to the new snapshot. Readers see the previous pointer with the previous quote or the new pointer with the new quote, never a mixture.
- **Rotation triggers.** Only a changed subscription opens a new connection: a positive non-usable status such as not `TRADING`. So do 23 hours of age or a provider `serverShutdown` message.
- **Transient check failures.** A failed instrument check keeps the subscription but makes that feed unavailable until a later check succeeds. A successful routine check never downgrades a `CONNECTED` feed, and publication never re-enables an `UNAVAILABLE` one.
- **Refresh cadence.** Routine clock and instrument refreshes start 45 seconds after the previous refresh *started*. One wait of up to a minute on the worker's own weight ceiling therefore cannot age a check past the 120-second usability bound. Waits on the IP-wide guard or on a provider back-off can follow one another; a check that expires fails closed, and the wait is recorded ([ExecPlan 0022](../plans/0022-reference-checks-and-rest-budget.md)).
- **Make-before-break order.** Rotation registers the new generation (epoch advance, no feed reset), then revalidates clock and instruments, then opens the replacement. The replacement's first frame cancels the old reader, and the old socket then closes in the background. Each symbol's pointers move with its first published new-generation quote, and no backfill runs during rotation.
- **Overlap bound.** At most two sockets exist, and a socket still closing counts. The replacement has seven seconds from the start of its open to deliver a first frame. The old socket's close is bounded by websockets' own two-second `close_timeout`, which aborts the transport; the worker never cancels a close. Overlap therefore stays under ten seconds.
- **Replacement failure.** A replacement that delivers nothing leaves the current generation in place and records `STREAM_REPLACEMENT_FAILED`. Retries wait 60 seconds, and after five failed attempts normal failure recovery takes over. A replacement that delivers but does not publish a quote for every symbol within five seconds of the cut fails the collection with its phase recorded.

One reader per socket timestamps frames immediately after `recv()`. The explicit application queue counts queued and in-flight frames, capped at 4,096 events or 8 MiB, with batches of at most 32 events/100 ms. A conservative maximum-frame reservation before receive prevents overlapping readers from exceeding the byte cap. Warning thresholds are 75% capacity or 250 ms oldest age; capacity exhaustion or age above one second makes current observations ineligible and closes collection. Readers treat a telemetry sample's oldest pending frame as still pending until the next sample, so the worker samples every 250 ms. A healthy writer's in-flight batch therefore never reads as a one-second lag. REST history ingestion takes the writer lock per 32-row chunk, so an hourly backfill delays stream publication by at most one chunk. Captured frames drain through the same parser/store path. Incidents record the known `captured_pending` count. If a drain fails, `captured_uncommitted` is unknown: some subgroups may already be durable, so pending frames are not an exact loss count. A completed drain establishes zero uncommitted captured frames; `unobserved_count` remains unknown. Separate writer/control executors, two-second statement and one-second lock deadlines keep lease/telemetry work independent. No private WebSocket buffer internals are used.

The one-second age is the age of the oldest frame that is not yet published, including the batch the writer is persisting. It bounds receipt-to-publication delay; it is not a count of waiting frames and not event-loop scheduling lag. [ExecPlan 0021](../plans/0021-publication-cost-at-density.md) keeps that delay independent of retained history:

- **Publication by identity.** The worker publishes the revisions it has just persisted by their identities, in the same fenced, serialized publication transaction as before. An identity stays held by the process until its publication commits, so a failed attempt is retried.
- **Recovery scan.** Finding *every* unpublished revision reads all stored observations and publications, so that scan never runs while frames wait. It runs once at each generation start, before streaming, under its own 20-second statement deadline. It publishes whatever an earlier generation or process persisted and left unpublished, including a persistence commit whose outcome the process never learned.
- **Confirmations.** A candle confirmation is published by the transaction that follows its write, and by the same generation-start scan.
- **Retention off the writer's thread.** A retention call that deletes takes hundreds of milliseconds. Retention runs on its own database thread and connection, so stream statements never queue behind it. Its 500-row calls, fence and rules are unchanged.
- **Unchanged.** Capture identity, receipt order inside a publication batch, sequence assignment, the watermark and pointer activation.

The `PerformanceDensity` phase measures the declared normal workload against up to seven days of stored full-density observations; its budgets are in [TESTING.md](TESTING.md).

[ExecPlan 0022](../plans/0022-reference-checks-and-rest-budget.md) keeps a healthy collection through routine reference checks. The quote-usability rule, the clock rule and the 120-second bound are unchanged.

- **A routine clock check has five outcomes.**
  - *Verified:* offset at most 1,000 ms and uncertainty at most 500 ms, with no recovery pending.
  - *Recovery pending:* a good measurement while the two good checks after a failed measurement are not complete.
  - *Imprecise:* the uncertainty is over 500 ms, and the measurement does not show the clock out of bounds.
  - *No measurement:* every sample's request failed.
  - *Out of bounds:* the offset is over 1,000 ms on a precise measurement, or beyond 1,000 ms by more than the uncertainty of an imprecise one.
- **An unmeasurable clock keeps the collection.** On *recovery pending*, *imprecise* and *no measurement* every quote is unusable at once: the feeds are `UNAVAILABLE` with `CLOCK_RECOVERY_PENDING`, `CLOCK_UNCERTAIN` or `CLOCK_CHECK_FAILED`. The socket, the generation, its epoch and the capture stay. The worker checks the clock again five seconds after each attempt ended, doubling that wait up to a minute while the request itself keeps failing, and runs nothing else until a check verifies it: no instrument check, no history, no conflict confirmation, no retention and no rotation. Usability returns through `CHECKING` and each feed's next published quote.
- **A wrong clock closes the collection.** A measurement that shows the clock out of bounds closes it as before, and a new generation needs a verified clock. A frame whose event time is more than two seconds ahead of its receipt, arriving while the clock is unverified, closes it too.
- **What a clock sample measures.** The uncertainty is half the time from writing the request on an open connection to the complete answer. The wait for the weight budget, the DNS lookup and the set-up of a connection come before that interval. A sample is one request attempt with a three-second deadline. A failed sample is dropped, and the check time is the time of the sample that was used.
- **Rotation.** A rotation that is due waits while the clock is unverified. A clock failure met inside a rotation's revalidation closes the feeds, abandons that replacement and keeps the current generation.
- **Conflict confirmation.** A confirmation request that fails, or whose answer is invalid, empty or not the one finalized candle asked for, is recorded as `CONFIRMATION_FAILED` and tried again at the next refresh. It ends neither the collection nor the process.
- **History.** A failed history request marks a checking or connected feed `DEGRADED`. It never re-opens an `UNAVAILABLE` one.
- **REST weight.** The worker's own requests stay under 120 weight in any 60 seconds, every attempt charged, or under the provider's declared allowance when that is lower. The declared allowance is the one-minute `REQUEST_WEIGHT` limit in the `rateLimits` array of an `exchangeInfo` answer. The IP-wide `X-MBX-USED-WEIGHT-1M` value is held under half that allowance. Until an allowance has been read, at the start of a process, it is held under the own ceiling. Above the guard requests wait; nothing is sent against the header.
- **Provider back-off.** After a 429 or 418 no request leaves before `Retry-After` has passed, whichever part of the worker asks. A waiting request ends at once when the worker stops.
- **Evidence.** `CLOCK_UNCERTAIN`, with the measured offset and uncertainty, or `CLOCK_CHECK_FAILED` opens an unverified interval. `CLOCK_UNVERIFIED` closes it with its start, end, number of checks and how it ended. `REST_WAIT` records a wait of 30 seconds or more, at most once in ten minutes, and `REST_BACKOFF` every 429 or 418 with its status.
- **Replay.** Quotes captured while the clock is unverified are stored and published, and are unusable live. Replay's reduced rule does not see feed state, so an analysis that needs them excluded uses the `CLOCK_UNVERIFIED` interval.

Finalized-candle conflicts remain missing from the usable projection until a fresh bounded REST request confirms an existing revision. Append-only confirmation evidence is separately published through the observation watermark; an earlier watermark cannot see a later resolution. Another conflicting final revision reopens the interval. Provisional ordering follows provider event time, so delayed events cannot replace newer captures. Each REST attempt, including a transport retry, consumes request weight; fixed destinations, the five-attempt limit of instrument and history requests and `Retry-After` remain enforced.

The database-owned retention procedure prunes unpinned quotes and provisional candles after seven days and finalized candles after 730 days, at most 500 observations and 700 orphan payloads per call (500 before [ExecPlan 0038](../plans/0038-faster-market-data-cleanup.md)). The worker schedules up to 80 bounded calls at startup and hourly, enough to retire the declared 27,000 observations/hour of five coins when eligible (32 calls for the two coins' 10,800 before M10's update 2). It records a deletion digest/count/replay-fidelity manifest; referenced pins, instrument snapshots and confirmed revisions prevent deletion. PostgreSQL deletion does not immediately shrink table/index files; physical space may await reuse or maintenance. The worker stops collection at its 12 GiB market-table cap, and the health view warns from 10 GiB (5 and 4 GiB before M10's update 2; seven days of two coins measured about 2.5 GB, so five need about 6.2 GB). These windows are operational retention policy for captured samples, not a full market replay guarantee. The declared long-duration throughput and storage measurements remain in [ExecPlan 0003](../plans/0003-market-data-charts.md).

[ExecPlan 0024](../plans/0024-retention-failure-keeps-the-worker.md) keeps the worker and its collection when the database ends a retention call. The function, its call size, the two deadlines, the fence and the retention rules are unchanged.

- **A failed call ends one attempt and nothing else.** A retention call can end with a database error: its two-second statement deadline, its one-second lock deadline, or any other error the database reports. The socket, the generation, its epoch and the capture stay, and no `COLLECTION_GAP` is recorded, because no collection ended. What the failed call had deleted is rolled back by the database; the calls before it stay committed.
- **A lost lease still stops the worker.** That includes the function's own lease check, which is reported as a lost lease and not as a failed call. An error that is not a database error still ends the process, as before.
- **Retry.** The pass is tried again at the first routine refresh at least 60 seconds later. The wait doubles, up to the normal hour, only for attempts that completed no call; an attempt that completed a call before it failed is followed by the 60 seconds again.
- **The bound.** No hour has more than 80 calls (32 before M10's update 2), failed ones included. A retry uses the calls that are left; when none is left it waits until the oldest call is an hour old.
- **Generation changes.** A rotation, or the start of a new collection, waits up to three seconds for a retention call that is still running. Such a call holds the lease row shared until it ends, and the new generation's epoch update must not wait for it under its one-second lock deadline.
- **Start-up.** The pass before the stream opens follows the same rule: a failed call lets the stream start, and the pass is tried again under the stream.
- **Evidence.** Every failed attempt is one incident. `RETENTION_FAILED` carries the error class, the SQLSTATE, the function line the database reports, the calls and records the attempt completed, the number of failed attempts and the wait. Once no call has completed for three hours the code is `RETENTION_STALLED`, so a retention that removes nothing stays among the incidents. `RETENTION_RECOVERED` closes the failures with their start, their end and their count. Retention's calls and its records use its own database thread, never the stream writer's.
- **What this does not do.** A retried call is the same size as the failed one: the function fixes 500 observations and 500 payloads, and a smaller call needs a change of the function ([the plan's §7](../plans/0024-retention-failure-keeps-the-worker.md#7-decisions-for-the-owner)).

[ExecPlan 0038](../plans/0038-faster-market-data-cleanup.md) (migration `0017`) changes where a call looks for orphan payloads, not what it may delete:

- **Payloads of the call's own observations.** A payload whose observation the call deletes goes in the same call when no other observation and no confirmation refers to it. A payload shared by two observations goes with the second.
- **A bounded sweep for the rest.** Each call also examines at most 200 payloads older than seven days, in receipt order after the position where the previous call stopped (`market_retention_cursor`, written only by the function), and deletes those nothing refers to: a payload stored without an observation, such as an invalid frame's, and the orphans the function of `0004` left. A payload that is kept is examined once, when the sweep passes it. A call therefore deletes at most 500 observations and 700 payloads. The function of `0004` examined every payload older than seven days in every call.
- **Indexes.** The five references to `market_observations` from M3 tables (`strategy_evaluations.bar_observation_id`, `orders.decision_quote_observation_id`, `orders.instrument_observation_id`, `fills.quote_observation_id`, `fills.instrument_observation_id`) are indexed, so a deleted observation's reference checks no longer scan those tables.
- **A collection gap names its slowest call.** Every `COLLECTION_GAP` (and its `market_collection_gap` log line) carries `slowest_call`: the stream path's slowest database call in the 30 seconds before the collection failed, a call still running included (its name, milliseconds, start and whether it was still running), and `retention_running`, whether a retention call was in flight.

## Ingestion responsibilities

Use a shared worker-owned subscription layer, not a separate exchange connection per browser or strategy. Collect instrument/filter snapshots, finalized 1h candles and sufficient warm-up/history, quotes for execution, and depth/trades only as required by the selected execution model. Add 4h inputs for an approved strategy variant. Record raw or losslessly normalized evidence sufficient for replay and quality audits.

Implement heartbeat, bounded timeouts/retries with jitter, reconnect/backoff, clock checks, duplicate suppression, rate-limit accounting and gap detection against current provider documentation [EX-02](REFERENCES.md#ex-02). Check snapshot/delta update IDs before trusting reconstructed depth. Backpressure must not silently discard events needed for accounting or fills.

Data freshness is determined at the engine decision/execution boundary, not by UI polling frequency. The initial quote-age proposal is in [RISK_POLICY.md](RISK_POLICY.md); its effectiveness must be tested on this PC and connection. A quote can be too stale even when the socket appears connected.

## Connection failure and recovery

On loss of required observations, stop new exposure and retain pending obligations. Display the affected gap and last trustworthy observation. A scheduled exit requiring unavailable prices cannot be marked filled. After reconnection, reconcile subscriptions, server clock, instruments, quotes/depth, open orders and account state before resuming.

Historical candle backfill repairs research/chart coverage; it does not establish that the forward trader was awake and executing during downtime. Do not fabricate missed orders at favorable historical prices. An offline counterfactual replay may be generated as a separate labeled research run.

## Retention and resources

Store durable financial events, experiment manifests and evidence references without silent pruning. Propose market-observation retention after measuring storage growth and PC resources. Compact/archive with checksums and a documented replay-fidelity policy. Full-depth, tick-by-tick indefinite retention is not a requirement for this first local app.

Before deleting source records used by an experiment, retain an immutable reproducible dataset or mark the experiment no longer fully reproducible. Never delete losing experiments to improve reported performance. Record ingestion lag, gaps, reconnects, rate-limit responses, schema errors and storage pressure.

## Broader market factors

At M6, an approved source can add primary-source announcements or scheduled events. Each feature needs an explicit hypothesis, timestamp semantics, out-of-sample evaluation and an ablation showing whether it adds information. The product must not claim to observe everything that affects prices.

No paid news, derivatives, on-chain, social sentiment or macro provider is enabled by default. Do not scrape a site or use an undocumented endpoint merely to fill the dashboard. External-source coverage and unknowns must be visible.

## Agent-driven evidence requests and event triggers

Broader approved evidence ingestion is now part of the M6 agent workflow, not unrestricted native model browsing. Implement source registry/permission preflight, scoped evidence search and source-refresh requests under [RESEARCH_WORKFLOWS.md](../RESEARCH_WORKFLOWS.md). A source stays disabled until its real interface, rights and timestamp behavior are verified. No specific news provider is assumed connected.

M6 uses recorded public quotes only, staged through new bounded `research_read` views without modifying an M1–M4 table or copying pilot evidence pins. Copy published collections, payloads, observations, confirmations and publications in dependency order. A store is append-only and versioned; replacement drops a whole unreferenced research database, never deletes the pilot's market rows. Bar-history research is out of scope. Deferred shadows carry their reduced replay-eligibility and downtime limitations; their results do not change live quote usability.

B4 implements that staging (IMPLEMENTED; verified on synthetic fixture rows only). A row is copied once its publication has been visible for 60 seconds, by publication sequence, in batches of at most 500 rows, never starting a batch between HH:58 and HH:07 UTC. Rows travel as PostgreSQL's own row text and are compared with their source after the write, so no value is re-encoded. A row a publication depends on (its instrument snapshot, a confirmed candle, a payload, a collection) is copied with it; a published row whose dependency is not published is skipped and recorded. A new store version starts at the oldest publication main still has within 21 days: final candles reach back that far, quotes only as far as main's 7-day retention. A quote gap of more than 60 seconds per symbol is recorded, as possible retention loss when it starts before that 7-day horizon. Staging reads the main database through `research_read` only and writes nothing there except its journal in the M6 catalog.

Persist a normalized source event ID and revision, publication time when supplied, local receipt/availability, affected scope, provenance and deterministic trigger rule. Repeated refreshes or duplicate syndicated reports must not repeatedly trigger the same trade workflow. Corrections invalidate affected analyses without rewriting original decisions. Market risk processing must continue independently of a slow external article fetch.

Numerical market data and narrative evidence have separate freshness/quality policies. Article age cannot make a quote valid, and a fresh quote cannot validate an unsupported news claim. Agents can request more permitted context or abstain; they cannot invent a source or choose an arbitrary historical availability time.

**M9 update 1 (IMPLEMENTED, not yet installed; [ExecPlan 0032 §5.1](../plans/0032-m9-news-and-events-the-agents-trade-on.md#51-free-feeds-update-1)).** Four free official feeds join the two release lists: the BLS release calendar, the BLS latest numbers, the Federal Reserve's monetary policy releases and the ECB's press releases and speeches. ## M3 execution use of sampled quotes

The statement above that quotes are "never executable prices" means they are never real or guaranteed executable prices. From M3, sampled level-1 quotes are the approved **modeled** execution input at fidelity `SAMPLED_L1_TICKER` ([PAPER_EXECUTION](PAPER_EXECUTION.md#m3-implementation-execplan-0011)); market reads keep `executable: false`, and every fill records its fidelity and quote provenance. The execution engine reads only published observations bound to a publication watermark and to the step's decision time (`available_at <= decided_at`), uses the same quote-usability predicate as `read.quote()` (`app/execution/eligibility.py`), and pins every referenced quote, candle and instrument snapshot in `market_evidence_pins`, so M2 retention skips them. M3 does not change the market worker or the retention function.

**Hourly quote gap (OBSERVED 2026-09-28):** Binance changes `MARKET_LOT_SIZE.maxQty` hourly, and the original M2 worker rotated its stream on every instrument change; the rotation timed out, leaving 50–85 s without quotes near the top of each hour. [ExecPlan 0012](../plans/0012-market-stream-continuity.md) fixes that and was deployed to the normal app on 2026-09-28; the M3 release includes the committed 0012 changes (`02f3790`) so the M3 rollout does not revert them. Whatever the cause of a gap, M3 handles it honestly: entries defer within the validity window, exposed positions record `PROTECTION_UNAVAILABLE` intervals and no price is invented.

**M4 evaluation capture (ExecPlan 0015).** While a run is live, the execution step records at the first step at or after each UTC hour one `evaluation_marks` row per symbol, and FLOW marks after activation and each posted top-up. Each mark copies the quote's bid, ask, quantities, times, quality and the instrument's lot step, tick and commission precision inline. Marks add no evidence pins and do not change M2 retention. Full replay beyond seven days needs the owner's weekly market-table dumps ([OPERATIONS](../OPERATIONS_M3_M7.md#m4-pilot-operations-execplan-0015), OD-14).

The unfiltered `exchangeInfo` was 5.9 MB on the owner's PC on 2026-10-20, over the 2 MiB cap, so the scan always names its candidates ([INTEGRATION_EVIDENCE](INTEGRATION_EVIDENCE.md#m10-update-1-the-coin-watchlist-and-the-notes--2026-10-20)).
- **What it keeps.** Each scan's entries (the API and the agents read the scans and entries, never the bars) with the provider's fields as received, and the finalized UTC daily bars it read (the open bar is dropped), in their own append-only tables ([DATA_CONTRACTS](DATA_CONTRACTS.md#m10-records-execplan-0033)). A daily bar or a book ticker of the scan is never a quote, a candle of the market tables, a mark or a fill input, and no coin of the watchlist is quoted, charted or traded by update 1.
- **Instrument checks.** A candidate is kept only when `exchangeInfo` lists it `TRADING` with `isSpotTradingAllowed` true and the USDT quote asset; the permission sets are kept as received.

## M10 update 2: five traded coins on one worker (ExecPlan 0033)
