# Operations, scheduling and recovery

## Local operating reality

The market/risk worker must be running to monitor observations and manage paper positions. Closing the browser does not stop it; stopping Docker, PC sleep, power loss or lost internet does. Display this limitation before activation and on the health page. Do not imply a local app offers unattended cloud availability.

Use a persistent worker with durable schedules/jobs in PostgreSQL. No in-memory browser timer, request handler, or model conversation is the authoritative scheduler. M0/M1 establish lifecycle; later milestones add domain schedules.

## Proposed cadence by responsibility

Market observations are processed as received, with freshness checks at every decision. Protective exits are evaluated against eligible fresh data independently of model cadence. Reference entry/trend signals use final 1h bars. The retained ADAPTIVE_SELECTOR baseline has a daily UTC checkpoint. AGENT_DRIVEN uses separately approved finalized-bar, source-update, position and scheduled-review triggers, with deduplication/cooldowns and bounded role workflows. Research jobs are independently budgeted and cannot starve risk monitoring.

Use unique schedule keys and durable leases to prevent duplicate runs after restart. Scheduler delivery can be at least once; financial effects remain idempotent. A missed checkpoint is marked missed or resumed under a predeclared policy, never backdated as if it ran on time.

## Run state

Represent experiment state explicitly: `DRAFT`, `READY`, `RUNNING`, `PAUSED`, `STOPPING`, `STOPPED`, `COMPLETED`, `FAILED`. Track pause causes separately, such as owner request, data gap, model unavailable, policy trigger or reconciliation failure. Clearing one cause does not clear all others.

Pause means no new exposure; existing approved exit mandates continue when observations allow. Stop-with-open-positions retains those obligations and requires explicit handling. Flatten is a requested modeled liquidation, not a guaranteed completed fill. Display unresolved positions after shutdown/failure.

## Recovery sequence

M2 market recovery is independent of M1 financial reconciliation. With collection enabled, the worker acquires the separate market lease, checks provider clock and instrument snapshots, backfills bounded finalized history, then subscribes to the combined public stream. It retains actual outage/incident evidence; restored history cannot imply the application observed the missing forward interval. A lease loss prevents stale publication. The market feed can be disabled while the owner/API/account remain available; existing quotes then age into unavailable state. M2 never opens a position, posts a fill or changes cash.

Under the [2026-09-28 owner decision](../../DECISIONS.md#2026-09-28--completed-milestones-must-run-locally), M2 completion authorizes deployment of the verified software to the normal local app. It does **not** authorize enabling public collection or later trading/agent capabilities. For a disposable check, use the guarded `PublicWorkerStart`, `PublicWorkerReport`, `PublicWorkerNetworkInterrupt` and `PublicWorkerStop` phases in [COMMANDS.md](../COMMANDS.md); their run manifest isolates database, secrets, network and volume. Interpret `CONNECTED` as transport state only. Read `quote_usable`, original source/receipt/availability times, quote/message ages, reference checks, collection identity, exact epoch and incidents before treating a displayed quote as current; a failed UI refresh marks cached values unusable. During an outage retain evidence/timestamps, restore the provider/database and require a new fenced generation, reference checks and a newly captured quote. The living plan distinguishes historical database recovery, injected rotation and bounded real network-interruption evidence.

A routine instrument change, such as Binance's roughly hourly `MARKET_LOT_SIZE.maxQty` recomputation, is adopted on the live socket without a new generation or gap. A scheduled rotation, a provider `serverShutdown` or a subscription change is make-before-break: quotes stay usable from the current generation until each symbol publishes a quote from the replacement ([MARKET_DATA.md](MARKET_DATA.md#implemented-m2-observation-boundary)). To audit rotations and adoptions:

- The `STREAM_ROTATION` incident records the trigger and pending generation.
- `STREAM_REPLACEMENT_FAILED` records a replacement that was abandoned while the current generation continued.
- The `COLLECTION_GAP` detail records the failure `phase` and original `failure_code`.
- Worker log events `market_rotation_completed`, `market_socket_closed` (close duration, close code, timeout), `market_instrument_adopted` and `market_feed_activated` record each step.

Each generation start publishes what an earlier generation or process left unpublished; the worker logs `market_publications_recovered` with the record count when there was any ([ExecPlan 0021](../plans/0021-publication-cost-at-density.md)). A rising `publication_lag_ms` in worker health with a small queue count means the database statements behind publication are slow, not that frames are piling up.

Reading an unverified clock and a REST wait ([ExecPlan 0022](../plans/0022-reference-checks-and-rest-budget.md)):

- **Feeds `UNAVAILABLE` with `CLOCK_UNCERTAIN`, `CLOCK_RECOVERY_PENDING` or `CLOCK_CHECK_FAILED`, on an unchanged collection and socket.** A routine clock check could not verify the clock, and the worker rechecks every five seconds. No action is needed. The `CLOCK_UNVERIFIED` incident that follows gives the interval and how it ended.
- **`CLOCK_UNCERTAIN` carries the measured `offset_ms` and `uncertainty_ms`.** An offset over 1,000 ms means the local clock was wrong: the collection was closed, and the host's time synchronisation needs a look.
- **`market_references_checked`, logged once per refresh,** lists each clock sample's parts in milliseconds: `wait_ms` for the weight budget, `dns_ms`, `connect_ms` and `exchange_ms`. It also gives the highest IP-wide weight seen (`ip_used_max`) beside the worker's own (`own_used`), the guard and the declared allowance. `market_clock_checked` gives the same parts for every check that did not verify.
- **`REST_WAIT`** names its reason. `ip_guard`: other clients on the same public IP used more than half the provider's allowance. `own_ceiling`: the worker's own requests reached 120 in a minute, which can happen around a restart. `provider_backoff`: see the next item.
- **`REST_BACKOFF` with status 429:** the provider asked for a pause, and the worker holds every request for the stated seconds. **With status 418** the provider has banned the IP for that time: tell the owner, and look for the other client on the IP.
- **`CONFIRMATION_FAILED`:** a candle conflict could not be confirmed this time and stays out of the usable projection. The next refresh tries again.

Reading a failed retention ([ExecPlan 0024](../plans/0024-retention-failure-keeps-the-worker.md)):

- **`RETENTION_FAILED`, then `RETENTION_RECOVERED` a minute or two later.** The database ended one retention call, and the next attempt completed the pass. The collection was never touched. No action is needed. The detail's `sqlstate` says which deadline it was: `57014` is the two-second statement deadline, `55P03` the one-second lock deadline.
- **`where`** is the function line the database reports. In the function of migration `0004_m2_completion`: line 10 selects the observations, 23 deletes their publications, 24 deletes the observations, 27 selects the orphan payloads, 39 deletes them and 43 writes the manifest.
- **`RETENTION_STALLED`.** No retention call has completed for three hours, and every further failed attempt is recorded under this code, once an hour at most. Quotes are not affected. The market tables grow until the 10 GiB warning and the 12 GiB stop (4 and 5 GiB before M10's update 2), so tell the owner: the same `sqlstate` and `where` in every record point at one statement or one lock.
- **A SQLSTATE of class 23** means a row refers to an observation that the retention rule wants to delete and that is not pinned. Retention then fails at the same row every time, and only a correction of that reference or of the rule ends it.
- **Log events.** `market_retention_failed` for every failed attempt, with `stalled`; `market_retention_recovered`; `market_retention_pruned` with the records an attempt removed, also a failed one; `market_retention_record_failed` when the incident itself could not be written.
- **Repeated `RETENTION_FAILED` with `calls` above zero** means retention works and is interrupted: each attempt removes rows before one call fails. It is behind, not stalled, and is tried again every minute within its 80 calls an hour.
- **`retry_in_seconds`** is the real wait. It is longer than the usual 60 seconds when the hour's 80 calls are used.
- **After a restart** the worker counts failed attempts from one again. The incidents of the earlier process stay.

`python -m app.market_data.continuity_probe --seconds N`, run in the market-worker container with `ASL_DB_ROLE=market_worker`, samples the published quote/health path once per second without writing; see [COMMANDS.md](../COMMANDS.md).

Queue telemetry older than six seconds or from an invalid lease is unknown. The UI shows age/capacity warnings, overload and the sample time. Collection gaps distinguish known pending-frame counts, unknown partial-commit outcomes and unknown network loss; a pending batch may already contain durable subgroups and must not be counted as exact loss. A failed database drain logs the error class and SQLSTATE without raw SQL or secrets. Capture/publication transactions hold a shared authority lock; expiry-only renewal is independent, while the unique authority key makes epoch/owner changes wait for those transactions. A database-local advisory lock serializes capture decisions and publication order. Worker statements/lock waits remain bounded. See [MARKET_DATA.md](MARKET_DATA.md) for queue, rotation, request and storage limits.

The M2 retention function removes expired unpinned captured samples in bounded batches and records deletion manifests. Check storage warnings and pinned references before resuming collection after a storage stop. Logical deletion does not immediately reduce PostgreSQL relation files; the seven-day sampled rehearsal measured this directly. Keep backups outside the database volume and preserve any observations accumulated after a backup before attempting forward repair.

The normal M0–M2 Compose configuration now sets CPU, memory and PID ceilings for web, API, PostgreSQL and the market worker, plus per-container `json-file` rotation; [Compose](../../../compose.yaml) is the canonical source for the values. These caps do not bound PostgreSQL data/WAL, protected backups, retained test volumes, image/build caches or total host disk use. The market relation warning/stop thresholds are therefore not whole-app free-space guarantees. Before a sustained run or upgrade, record free space on both the Windows host and Docker filesystem, database relation/WAL and backup sizes, and Docker image/volume/log usage. Retain pinned and financial evidence during any low-space response; a cleanup or backup-retention decision must identify the exact disposable resources and preserve recovery copies. Recheck actual container limits and rotation after recreation rather than treating source Compose values as deployment evidence.

Before each owner-environment upgrade, create a protected backup. The completion API requires `0004_m2_completion`; unchanged M1 or initial M2 API images expect older schemas and are not compatible fallbacks. Use a schema-compatible image with market collection disabled. Do not downgrade or delete market/financial history. Disposable rehearsals compare financial fingerprints through M1→head and 0003→0004, then verify dump/restore fingerprints and reconciliation on separate databases. See the exact [run record](../plans/0003-market-data-charts.md); those isolated rehearsals are not backups of owner data.

On startup, verify schema/config, acquire a fenced execution authority, reconcile journal/projections/reservations/orders, assess data gaps and get valid current market snapshots. Re-establish runtime capability status separately. Restore latched policy pauses and original position mandates. Resume only when the relevant readiness/owner requirements pass.

After a DB timeout, reconcile by durable IDs before retrying an unknown outcome. After a model timeout, cancel/kill the child process and reject late output. After a feed reconnect, rebuild required stream state before enabling fills. No historical replay is posted into the main forward ledger to conceal downtime.

M1 specifically reconciles the account journal/projection/reservations on API startup and on the authenticated reconcile route. A mismatch latches `reconciliation_failed` and `paused`; startup never rewrites a projection. The owner UI preserves an uncertain funding key for authoritative lookup. A `PENDING_VALUATION` top-up has no spendable cash and requires an explicit owner retry or cancellation. There is no worker, market mark or position recovery in M1.

## Milestone completion deployment

**Since M8 (ExecPlan 0031 §4.4), every update is one double-click:** [the update file](#the-update-file-m8). The steps below are what it does; a thread no longer runs them by hand.

For every authorized M-numbered implementation milestone, the normal local application must run the latest accepted milestone code before reporting full delivery. The owner's standing authorization covers routine local software deployment; it does not grant runtime agents deployment authority. A planning request or an explicit owner hold does not trigger deployment.

1. Confirm the normal Compose project, database, persistent volumes, current image IDs/schema and approved operating settings. Match the release to the tested source commit or source manifest. Keep isolated test fixtures out of owner data.
2. Preserve the existing database and secrets. Before an upgrade, create a protected timestamped database backup outside its volume, record its checksum, and verify it can be restored in isolation for schema changes. Keep the previous image identities and a schema-compatible fallback. Never use `down -v`, reset owner state or replace history with test data.
3. Build the accepted release in Docker with frozen dependencies. Generate only missing required local secrets; retain existing values. Quiesce affected writes according to the approved operating state, run one forward migration owner, and rebuild/recreate the affected normal services. Preserve protection/maintenance requirements for future active positions.
4. Verify actual running image IDs/source provenance, schema readiness, reconciliation where applicable, and the normal URL's pages. Since [ExecPlan 0042](../plans/0042-no-owner-sign-in.md) there is no sign-in: the app funds its account and switches its agents on by itself, and a deployment test changes nothing that is the owner's (top-ups, the off switch, Pause, Stop, Start); use isolated acceptance evidence for those.
5. Leave the normal app healthy and running. Preserve existing settings, safety latches and authorizations; installing software must not silently enable collection, trading, agents, research, models or paid calls. Stop only task-created verification resources, then remove them as [DOCKER](DOCKER.md#cleaning-up-disposable-resources) says. Evidence files and recovery data stay.
6. Record commands/results, backup location/checksum, source commit/image IDs, schema, URL, operating state and any remaining blocker in the current ExecPlan, STATUS and COMMANDS. Publish the reviewed changes according to the owner's Git delivery instructions. If the running app cannot be updated safely or verified, report deployment blocked and do not describe the milestone as fully delivered.
7. Once the deployment is verified, remove what it superseded, as [DOCKER](DOCKER.md#cleaning-up-disposable-resources) says: rollback tags older than this deployment's, untagged images and the build cache. Keep the latest rollback tag of every service and any image a running experiment's workers are pinned to.

### The update file (M8)

`update-app.cmd` in the owner's main folder (a copy of `tools/update.cmd`, ignored by Git) runs `tools/update.ps1` as GitHub's `main` holds it, with Windows PowerShell, on that folder. The owner double-clicks it; it asks nothing and the window stays open at the end. It replaced the M5 to M7 deploy and activation scripts (removed in ExecPlan 0034) for routine updates, and enables no identity, sets no flag and approves nothing.

1. **The folder.** With an uncommitted change to a tracked file it stops and names the files, changing nothing. Otherwise it puts the folder on `main` and fast-forwards it to `origin/main`; a local `main` with commits GitHub lacks stops it. When the fast-forward brought a newer copy of the update file, that copy runs instead.
2. **Preflight** (read-only): Docker, postgres, api and web running and the api ready, the schema, and each running service's `ASL_*_ENABLED` flags equal to what the env file renders. Since [ExecPlan 0060](../../plans/0060-spot-trading-only.md), while the database still holds the futures account (before migration `0041`), that account must be flat (no open position, no pending funding payment, nothing reserved); otherwise the update stops with nothing changed and says how to close it (Settings' Stop and close, then the update again).
3. **Backup and rollback tags.** A protected backup of the main database with its SHA-256 (refused without room for it), and `<project>-<service>:before-update` tags on the images that run. After a run that stopped once it had set its tags, those tags are kept, since they still name the release before it.
4. **Build** every image of the app (never postgres).
5. **Migrate** the main database to the head of the built code (three bounded attempts). When the new api accepts the schema that is there, the api and then the web are replaced first. The api always comes before the web, whose health check reads the home page and so the api: on 2026-10-25 a new web that needed the new api failed its health check against the old one, and the update stopped there ([ExecPlan 0042 §13](../plans/0042-no-owner-sign-in.md#13-progress)).
6. **Recreate** each running service whose image changed, or whose settings changed (Compose's `config --hash` against the container's `com.docker.compose.config-hash` label; when Compose gives no hashes, only the image counts and the run says so), with `--no-deps` and its flags unchanged (api, web, the market, execution and evaluation workers, the agents' egress, runner and worker, then any other); a recreated agent worker must record its start and an execution worker hold its lease. The source fetcher is recreated last, so a fault there leaves the main app installed. Stopped services stay stopped. Then the containers of the retired research services are stopped and removed by id, and their network by its id once its labels show it is this app's ([DOCKER](DOCKER.md#research-services-retired-execplan-0035), ExecPlan 0035); the research stores are no longer migrated. Since [ExecPlan 0060](../../plans/0060-spot-trading-only.md) the services are the api, the web and the market and execution workers; before a migration the new api needs first, the two workers and the running containers of the retired services stop (migration `0041` takes each archived table's exclusive lock) and are started again by their ids if the migration does not apply. After the recreate, the containers of the retired services (the research services of ExecPlan 0035; the agents' worker, runner, egress and sign-in services, their test services, the source fetcher and the evaluation worker of ExecPlan 0060) are stopped and removed, each found by its exact Compose labels and removed by id, never with `-f`, and their networks by id; their volumes and the secret files stay.
7. **Verify:** the folder still at `origin/main`, each running service on its built image, the api ready, the schema at the head, reconciliation `OK`, the pages `/`, `/trades`, `/decisions`, `/markets` and `/settings` answering 200 (before ExecPlan 0039 update 2 part B: `/`, `/positions`, `/orders`, `/decisions`, `/markets`, `/coins` and `/settings`), no container or network of a retired research service left, and the agents' switch as it was (on is `ENABLED` or `PROBE_ONLY`). Since [ExecPlan 0060](../../plans/0060-spot-trading-only.md) the pages are `/`, `/trades`, `/backtest` and `/settings`, and a read-out that never stops the update follows: Bitcoin's spot feed and its daily history, the spot account, its run, the rule's newest reading and the backtest.
8. **Record** `update.json` beside the backup (`update-failed.json` after a stop) and print the release notes of the merges since the last completed update (or M7 deployment).
9. **Tidy:** delete each branch that `main` contains through a merge (never `main`, never a branch that only points into `main`'s own history, never one with commits `main` lacks) on GitHub, with a lease on the tip it checked so a branch pushed to since is kept, and in the folder, then list local branches with unmerged work. Then delete the database backups older than the newest three (never this run's, nor those of runs that stopped since the last completed one, whose images the rollback tags may still name), and remove this app's images that no service of the update uses, by exact name, never forced: old rollback tags, retired services, the test and tool images (a test run builds them again) and untagged builds ([ExecPlan 0034](../plans/0034-app-cleanup.md)). A failure here is reported; the update is done by then.

Before each change it waits by itself while the clock is near the hourly bar close (HH:55 to HH:05 UTC), and it keeps the PC awake while it runs. Any failed step stops the run where it is, records `FAILED`, and prints what it replaced, the schema, the backup and the record; nothing reports success after a failed step. A stopped update is fixed forward: the fix comes as a new update, and the next run continues from what is installed. Its transcript is `update-<time>.log` under the backup root (`.cache/deployments`).

**A new policy version** (from update 3, [RUNTIME_AGENTS](../RUNTIME_AGENTS.md#m8-runtime-the-main-run-the-app-keeps-going-execplan-0031)). An update that ships one needs nothing more from the owner. As soon as the main account holds no position or open order, the app stops the main run without flattening and creates and starts the run of the new version, within about a minute. A position open at the update stays with the old run, managed by the agents under the old version until it closes; the switch follows then. The update's verify step does not wait for the switch; Settings shows the run the agents trade, and its page who created and started it.

**The read-out** (from M9 update 1, [ExecPlan 0032 §5.1](../plans/0032-m9-news-and-events-the-agents-trade-on.md#51-free-feeds-update-1)). After the verify step, the update file prints, read-only, one line per source version (`not in force`, `in force, not fetched yet`, or its newest fetch: outcome, time, items parsed, new and rejected, and on a failure the HTTP status and the media type received) and one line for the newest forward run (`Main run <id>: <state> on <policy>`). While the source fetcher runs and a source in force has no fetch yet, it waits up to two minutes for one. The same values go into the record's `steps.read_out`. It never fails the update: a value it cannot read is printed as not read. A feed that fails keeps failing visibly in this read-out and in Settings' health table ("News and official releases" reads `degraded`, or `unavailable` when every feed fails); the next update fixes it.

From M10's update 1 it also prints the newest coin scan and the notes written; from update 2 one line per traded coin (`Coin ZECUSDT: CONNECTED, newest quote at 09:12:03 UTC`, or `no quote yet`), waiting up to two minutes for a coin with no quote, and, while the main run is on an earlier operating policy, that the app moves it to `AGENT_POLICY_V3` by itself once the account holds no position.

**The agent worker's healthcheck** (from M9 update 2, [ExecPlan 0032 §5.3](../plans/0032-m9-news-and-events-the-agents-trade-on.md#53-what-came-of-it-update-2)). Docker runs `python -m app.agents.health` every 5 seconds with a 3-second timeout. Until update 2 that check loaded the whole agent runtime, recompiling it each time because the app user may not write bytecode, and on the owner's PC it timed out while the worker ran normally, so Docker showed `agent-worker` as unhealthy from 2026-10-11 on. The check now reads the worker's heartbeat file without loading the runtime, and the image compiles its bytecode once when it is built. It still reports unhealthy when the heartbeat is more than 15 seconds old. Nothing restarts the worker on an unhealthy status.

### M11: the futures app's clean start (ExecPlan 0039)

The owner allowed a clean database for the futures app on 2026-10-23 ([0039 §5.1](../plans/0039-bitcoin-futures-on-paper.md#51-a-clean-start)). The update file does it once, by itself, on the first run that installs update 2 part B: when the main database has no `futures_runs` table. Nothing is deleted.

1. **Preflight** records that a clean start is due, checks the database name, and refuses when a database `<name>_before_futures` is already there.
2. After the usual backup, the backup is **copied** to `before-futures/` under the backup root (`.cache/deployments/before-futures/`) and its SHA-256 checked; the tidy step never deletes that folder.
3. At a quiet time (HH:05 to HH:55 UTC, with 10 minutes' room), the running services that connect to the database **stop**, the sessions still open are closed, and the database is **renamed** `<name>_before_futures` in the same volume (three tries). An **empty** database of the old name is created and migrated from the start to the head with `python -m app.research.migrate --keep-logins`. The identities belong to the cluster, and the owner's activations gave the source and research identities a login, while migration `0008` adopts those two only when they cannot log in. So the migration takes their logins and gives them back inside its own one transaction: a failed migration changes no login, and one that commits leaves each login as it found it. The record names the logins it kept (`logins_kept`).
4. Every service it stopped is **recreated** on the empty database, even where its image did not change, the api before the web, whose health check reads the api. The verify step requires the old database to be there and the agents' switch `DISABLED`, as on any new database; the read-out says "Futures account: not set up yet" with the owner's next steps. Since [ExecPlan 0042](../plans/0042-no-owner-sign-in.md) the api funds the empty database's account when it starts and the agent worker switches the agents on, so the verify step accepts the switch in its first state or switching on, and the read-out shows the account the app funded.
5. **A failure before the migration is done** undoes it: the new database is renamed `<name>_futures_failed_<time>`, the old one gets its name back, and each stopped service starts again in the container it had, by the container's id (`docker start`). Compose's own `start` refuses a service whose one-shot `migrate` dependency has no container (OBSERVED on the owner's PC on 2026-10-24). The undo checks that each one runs and records them (`started_again`), so the app runs as before. A failure after it stops the update as any failure does, with the old database still renamed and the copy in `before-futures/`.
6. **The next run after a stopped clean start** reads the newest update record since the last completed one. The services that record's clean start stopped (or its preflight found left stopped) and its undo did not start again count as running: they are recreated on their new images with the others. The preflight does not ask for a running api when the api is one of them, and stops before any change when one of them has no container.

The first install of part B stopped at step 3 on 2026-10-24 and its undo could not start the services again; what happened and the fix are in [0039 §15](../plans/0039-bitcoin-futures-on-paper.md#15-progress). The second, with the fix, stopped at step 3's rename: a session on the PC started the stopped services again between the preflight and step 3, so they were still connected; its undo started every service again. Nothing else acts on the app's containers while an update runs. The third, on 2026-10-25, did the clean start and stopped at the agent worker, whose image lacked the trader's prompt; the next run finishes it as step 6 says.

What changes for the owner: everything before the update (the five coins, the spot runs, decisions and the agents' history) stays in `<name>_before_futures` and in the copied backup; the app starts empty. The database identities are the cluster's, so they keep their logins; the owner's account starts unset. **After the update the owner** opens <http://localhost:3000>, sets up the owner with the one-time setup key (`secrets\owner_setup_key` in the app folder) and a passphrase, confirms the 10,000 virtual USDT, and turns the agents on in Settings; the first run starts by itself once the account is funded, and the trader once the agents' check passes. **Since [ExecPlan 0042](../plans/0042-no-owner-sign-in.md) none of these steps exists:** after the update that installs it, the api contributes the 10,000 virtual USDT itself when it starts, the agent worker switches the agents on, the first run starts once the account is funded and the trader once the agents' check passes. The owner does nothing; the setup key file stays in `secrets\`, read by nothing.

**Rollback** (PROPOSED, not run; prepared as a file for the owner if ever needed): stop the app's services, rename the futures database aside and `<name>_before_futures` back, start the `before-update` rollback tags of the images, and check reconciliation and the pages. ## Monitoring and incident record

Expose uptime, worker heartbeat, execution lease/fencing status, ingestion lag/gaps, clock disagreement, ledger reconciliation, pending exits, task queues, model auth/quota failures, disk/storage pressure, backup age and last restore test. Use measured values; no fake green health indicators.

Incident records identify first/last affected time, cause confidence, impacted symbols/experiments, financial uncertainty, response, recovery evidence and remaining limitations. Alerts live in the local UI/logs initially; external notifications require a later approved provider/configuration.

## Backup and restore

Back up PostgreSQL plus immutable experiment manifests/datasets needed for reproducibility. Define retention and storage after measuring local volume; keep a copy outside the database volume. Treat auth secrets separately with explicit encrypted/permission-controlled handling. A normal report export must never include Codex auth.

Provide a containerized restore procedure into an isolated instance and verify ledger reconciliation and artifact checksums there. Do not test restoration by overwriting the owner's only running database. Record dates/results of actual tests. Changing/deleting volumes requires explicit authorization, never routine coding cleanup.

For M1, stop financial mutations and use `pg_dump -Fc` on the named PostgreSQL database, preserve the dump outside the database volume with controlled local permissions, restore to a **new** database with `createdb` and `pg_restore`, then compare the account row, funding/journal counts, posted cash and reconciliation state. Validate the restored API against that database before considering operational recovery. The 2026-09-27 rehearsal used disposable `asl_m1_browser_0927b` and `asl_m1_restore_0927b` databases; source and restore matched on 12,200 virtual USDT, 122 units, NAV/high-water 100, two funding requests, two journal transactions and four postings. This rehearsal did not overwrite owner data or establish a general backup retention policy.

## Upgrade and future hosting

Before upgrades, preserve compatible backups, review schema/library/skill changes, run integration/financial regression tests, and record version transitions. New model versions may change outputs and therefore require evaluation segmentation.

Hosting later requires fresh deployment, authentication, TLS, monitoring, data rights and resource planning. Real Binance execution is a separately authorized project with new keys/permission/risk/testing requirements, not a configuration flip in this release.

## Agent workflows, priority and revocation

Since [ExecPlan 0035](../plans/0035-a-simpler-app-that-trades-by-itself.md#42-research-off-in-the-agent-worker) research is off: the agent worker starts no research workflow, scheduled research or promotion, and ends what was open when it takes the lease ([AUTONOMY_MATRIX](AUTONOMY_MATRIX.md#execplan-0035-research-off)). What follows on research is the design it was built to.

Persist workflow/step/tool state and available-at timestamps; use the enums in [RUNTIME_AGENTS.md](../RUNTIME_AGENTS.md). Waiting for a research job or review is durable state, not an open request thread or repeated model polling. Long research runs belong to `research-worker` with bounded resources, never the high-priority protection loop.

Priority classes are code-owned: protection/reconciliation first, market continuity next, eligible main decision work next, research/reports last. Reserve capacity for infrastructure tasks. Model-generated urgency cannot move a research job into protection priority. Enforce per-source/event dedupe and coalesce redundant low-priority events without discarding critical raw observations.

Terminate/cancel a workflow and its descendants on deadline, quota/budget exhaustion, permission revocation or superseding material state. A cancellation does not erase completed side effects. Reconcile any raced accepted orders/fills and cancel only unfilled discretionary entries permitted by current broker state. Reacquire state after restart rather than blindly executing a stored pending tool call.

Keep transient availability blocks distinct from latched pauses. Actual model/feed recovery may clear only that transient block under unchanged policy; owner/risk/integrity/revocation latches require the defined review/resume flow. No model can self-resume them. Include permission epoch and current authority in every commit check.

Track per-role queue delay, step/tool counts, cache/dedupe hits, inference latency, failed/expired reviews, unavailable evidence, proposed/accepted/rejected actions, budget headroom, incomplete experiments and last successful feedback review. Store actual usage only; subscription quotas and reset times can be unknown. Alerts remain local UI/log records, not a guarantee of notification while the PC is off.

Operator runbook must cover: disable agent workflows without disabling protection; stop research; revoke a policy/tool/source; reconcile unknown intent outcome; restore recorded evidence; perform owner-controlled reauthentication; and resume only after the appropriate checks. None of these commands may expose credentials or delete losing trials.
