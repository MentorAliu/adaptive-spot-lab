## 2. The owner's question: what decides how often futures should trade

More often is not better by itself. The right pace is the one where what a trade can be expected to earn is larger than what it costs, and the app can still decide well. What decides it:

1. **Costs per trade.** Every paper order is a market order, so it pays Binance's taker fee on entry and again on exit; Binance's fee page shows 0.05% taker for a regular account as its example ([REF-F1](#7-official-sources)), so about 0.10% of the position per round trip, plus the bid-ask spread and slippage. Costs are the same per trade whatever the timeframe, so ten trades cost ten times one.
2. **Noise against signal.** A price move over a short time is mostly noise. Typical moves grow roughly with the square root of time: a 15-minute bar moves about half as much as an hour, a 1-minute bar about an eighth, while the fee stays the same. At a minute or two, a typical move is no larger than the round-trip cost, so most trades would start behind and there is little left to predict. (A rule of thumb, `INFERRED`; before building, BTC's actual moves per timeframe are measured from Binance's public 1-minute candles, §9.)
3. **Funding.** A perpetual's holders pay or receive funding at each funding time, every 8 hours by default (00:00, 08:00, 16:00 UTC): position value at the mark price times the funding rate; longs pay shorts when it is positive ([REF-F2](#7-official-sources)). Short trades that close before a funding time pay none; longer holds pay or earn it.
4. **Leverage and liquidation.** Leverage multiplies profits, losses and fees against the margin. With isolated margin, the price must move against a position by about 1/leverage (less the maintenance margin) to liquidate it: about 50% at 2x, about 10% at 10x, about 1% at 100x (`INFERRED` from Binance's formula, [REF-F3](#7-official-sources)). At high leverage ordinary noise liquidates; at 1–2x with a stop, liquidation is a far-away backstop.
5. **Speed of the agent and its limits.** One decision is one or more model calls of tens of seconds or more. Under Codex, the 12:00 UTC bar's decision on 2026-10-21 started at 12:06 and the one after the brief at 12:16 ([0033 §14](0033-m10-the-agents-look-beyond-btc-and-sol.md#14-progress)). Each call also counts against the owner's ChatGPT Codex allowance, which the owner's own Codex use shares; it ran out several times on 2026-10-19 and 10-20 with today's agents. A 1-minute pace is out of reach for an AI agent; 5 minutes is possible but uses three times what 15 minutes uses.
6. **Paper fills flatter fast trading.** The app fills at the best price it observed; a real order meets latency and moving prices. The faster the trading, the larger that gap and the less a paper result says.

**Recommendation (PROPOSED):** code protects every second; the agent decides every 15 minutes and at once after a sharp move; at most 24 entries a UTC day. Faster paces stay possible later, once the app's own results show trades earn more than their costs.

#### 5.2.1 Part A as built (2026-10-24)

Update 2 comes in two installable parts ([§9](#9-implementation-sequence)). Part A collects the perpetual's data and shows it on Markets while the spot app keeps trading; part B is the futures account and the trader.
- **Streams.** Binance now routes its futures streams by path, and a connection receives only the streams of its own path, so the feed holds two: `/public/stream?streams=btcusdt@depth5@500ms` and `/market/stream?streams=btcusdt@markPrice@1s/btcusdt@kline_1m` ([INTEGRATION_EVIDENCE](../contracts/INTEGRATION_EVIDENCE.md#execplan-0039-part-a-the-btcusdt-perpetuals-public-data--2026-10-24)). The book is the partial depth stream's five best levels each 500 ms instead of `bookTicker`: the book ticker pushes every change, an unbounded rate for a worker limited to one CPU beside the spot collection, while two snapshots a second are plenty for 15-minute decisions and per-second protection, and the levels behind the best price let part B's fills walk the book. Each connection is renewed before Binance's 24 hours and after 30 s of silence.
- **Where it runs.** Inside the market worker, beside the spot collection, on its own tasks, database threads and client (its own weight count, `MAX_BODY` 8 MiB for the futures `exchangeInfo`, which takes no symbol). The feed waits for its schema, and any failure of it is recorded and retried, never stopping the spot collection (a test makes the feed itself fail and checks that the spot worker still runs).
- **Clock and instrument.** The futures server time is measured every minute (offset at most 1 s, uncertainty at most 0.5 s); `exchangeInfo` every 30 minutes, a new row whenever BTCUSDT's entry differs. A frame from more than 2 s ahead of the clock is dropped and the clock measured again.
- **What it stores** (migration `0019`, [DATA_CONTRACTS](../contracts/DATA_CONTRACTS.md#m11-records-execplan-0039)): one quote (the five levels a side) and one mark price with the funding rate per second, the newest of that second, kept 7 days; every 1m candle, a final one never changed; the settled funding rates; the feed's state and incidents. History is filled from REST to 7 days back and checked every 5 minutes; a settled funding rate is asked from 15 s after each funding time until it arrives, and its absence after 15 minutes is recorded `FUNDING_RATE_LATE`.
- **State.** `CONNECTED`; `DEGRADED` when the newest quote or mark is over 5 s old; `UNAVAILABLE` when a stream is down or the clock or the instrument is unverified; the API also says `FEED_SILENT` when the feed's heartbeat is over 10 s old.
- **Reads.** `GET /v1/perp/market` and `GET /v1/perp/candles` ([API_CONTRACTS](../contracts/API_CONTRACTS.md#m11-update-2-part-a-the-btcusdt-perpetuals-market-data-execplan-0039)); Markets shows a card above the coins with the state, the mark, bid and ask, the funding rate and the next funding time, the last settled rate, and the chart (1m, 5m, 15m, 1h; a candle with a missing or open minute drawn in the provisional colour, a missing candle left as a gap).
- **Volume** (Q06): two rows a second, 172,800 a day. OBSERVED on the synthetic feed of the browser check (366 quotes, 363 marks): about 490 bytes a quote row and 135 a mark row in the table, so about 55 MB a day and under 0.4 GB for the 7 days kept, `INFERRED` for Binance's own numbers of the same length; the 1m candles add about 1,440 small rows a day, kept. The real size is read on the PC after part A.

### 5.3 The paper futures account (money path)

- **One position at a time**, long or short, isolated margin, one-way mode. Margin = notional / leverage, reserved from the wallet in the entry's transaction.
- **Fills:** market orders at the best ask (buy) or bid (sell) of a quote at most 2 s old, plus a labeled 1 bps slippage scenario; no fill without such a quote (rule 11). Quantity rounded down to the step size; refused under the minimum notional.
- **Fees:** the taker rate read from Binance's fee table when built and recorded with its date ([REF-F1](#7-official-sources); the FAQ's example is 0.05%); no BNB discount.
- **Funding:** at each funding time, for a position open then: size × mark price at that time × the settled rate from `fundingRate`; a positive rate is paid by a long and received by a short. Until the settled rate is read, the payment is recorded pending and the valuation says so; nothing is guessed.
- **Liquidation:** Binance's isolated-margin formula with BTCUSDT's first-tier maintenance margin rate and amount, read from Binance's leverage and margin table when built and recorded with its date ([REF-F3](#7-official-sources)). The engine closes the position when the mark price crosses it, and the margin is lost as Binance would take it. An entry whose stop is not well inside the liquidation price is refused.
- **Ledger:** new event kinds for margin reserved and released, trading fee, realized P&L, funding paid and received, and liquidation, each exact NUMERIC, posted only by accounting, with reconciliation extended to margin and funding. Equity = wallet + unrealized P&L at the mark price; NAV stays unitized, and top-ups stay neutral.
- **Protection in code, every second:** the stop (on the bid for a long, the ask for a short), the take-profit, the time exit and liquidation, independent of the agent. A gapped stop can lose more than planned (tested).
- Migration `0018_futures`: the instrument, the futures fills, positions, margin and funding records, the event kinds, `FUTURES_POLICY_V1` and generation 7 (§5.5).

#### 5.3.1 Part B's design (2026-10-24)

Written before building part B, from the money code as it is (`app.accounting`, `app.execution`), so the review can hold the code to it; what the build changes is added under [§15](#15-progress). Migration `0020_futures`.
- **A domain of its own.** The futures account has its own records and its own engine, `app.futures`, so nothing of the spot broker's orders, positions, runs (`experiments`) or valuations is reused or changed in meaning; the spot code stays, unused on the clean volume, until update 3. Shared with the spot app: `account_state` (cash is the futures wallet; `reserved` is the margin of the open position), `funding_requests`, the owner's passphrase and sessions, `reservations` (one `USDT` row per position holds its margin, so the reserved-cash check of reconciliation holds as it is), the journal and reconciliation (extended), the execution lease and its fencing epoch, `execution_incidents`.
- **Definitions.** `FUTURES_POLICY_V1` (the values of §5.4) and `FUTURES_COST_V1` (the taker rate, the slippage scenario, BTCUSDT's maintenance tier and the liquidation rule, each with its source and the date read) are code-owned documents seeded into `execution_definitions` with their hashes, as the spot policies are; the worker starts only when the stored rows match the code, and each run records both hashes as `SYSTEM`.
- **Runs.** `futures_runs`: `RUNNING`, `PAUSED` (a cause open), `STOPPING` (no entries; the position keeps its stop, take-profit and time exit) and `STOPPED`, at most one not stopped. Causes: `OWNER` (the owner's Pause, cleared only by the owner's Resume), `DAILY_LOSS` (cleared by `SYSTEM` at the next UTC day's reference, as M8), `INTEGRITY` (cleared when reconciliation passes, as M8) and `DRAWDOWN`, which stops the run once flat (M8's rule). The first run starts by itself once the owner has funded the account; after the owner's Stop or a drawdown stop, nothing starts until the owner's "Start trading". The owner's requests (`futures_owner_requests`: Pause, Resume, Stop with or without closing the position, Start) are written by the API and applied by the engine in its fenced step, each answered once. A run's drawdown is measured from its own high-water NAV, which starts at its first NAV and never falls: a new run after a drawdown stop is a new evaluation episode ([RISK_POLICY](../../RISK_POLICY.md) "A separately approved new experiment can define a new evaluation episode"), and the account's own high-water NAV and every earlier run stay as they were.
- **Positions and fills.** `futures_positions` (one `OPEN` at most; side, leverage, quantity, entry, margin, stop, take-profit, liquidation price, time exit, and at close the exit, the realized P&L, fees and funding) and `futures_fills` (entry, exit and liquidation; the walked book levels and the quote's second are copied into the fill, because quotes are deleted after 7 days). Database guards: identity, side, leverage, entry, margin, take-profit, liquidation price and time exit never change; the stop only tightens; a closed position never changes.
- **Ledger.** `futures_ledger_events`, append-only: `MARGIN_RESERVED` and `MARGIN_RELEASED` move the reserved margin and post nothing; `TRADING_FEE` (OWNER_CASH −fee, EXPENSE +fee), `REALIZED_PNL` (OWNER_CASH ±, the new book account TRADING_PNL ∓), `FUNDING_PAID` and `FUNDING_RECEIVED` (OWNER_CASH ∓, the new book account FUNDING ±) and `LIQUIDATION` (OWNER_CASH −margin, TRADING_PNL +margin) each post one journal transaction whose new source column `futures_event_id` the execution identity alone may write. Reconciliation adds: every posting event has exactly its postings and every other none; the reserved margin equals the open position's; each position's fees and P&L equal its events; each fill has its events; each posted funding row has its event; and, from the money-path review (section 5.3.2), each fee, margin, realized P&L and funding amount recomputed from its own row.
- **Equity.** Wallet (cash) plus the unrealized P&L at the newest mark price at most 5 s old; without one while a position is open the valuation is `INVALID` and nothing that needs it acts (a top-up stays pending, as with a spot holding without a fresh bid). NAV stays unitized; a top-up issues units at the pre-flow NAV and moves no P&L, high-water mark or latch.
- **The money math, exact Decimal.** Margin = notional ÷ leverage, rounded up to 0.00000001. Fee = quantity × fill price × the taker rate, rounded up. Realized P&L = quantity × (exit − entry) for a long and × (entry − exit) for a short, rounded down. Funding at a funding time T for a position open at T = quantity × the settled mark at T × the settled rate; a long pays a positive rate and a short receives it; a payment is rounded up and a receipt down; until the rate is settled the payment is `PENDING` and the valuation lists it. Liquidation price: Binance's isolated-margin formula for one-way mode with BTCUSDT's first maintenance tier, fixed at entry; the margin is lost in full when the mark price crosses it, and an exit fill at or beyond it is a liquidation too (an isolated position loses at most its margin).
- **Fills.** Market orders walk the five levels of the newest quote at most 2 s old, plus the labeled 1 bps slippage scenario against the order; the quantity is rounded down to the step. No quote that fresh, no fill: an entry is refused, an exit waits and its incident is recorded. An entry is also refused under the minimum quantity or notional, or when the five levels cannot fill it.
- **Sizing, in code.** The agent picks the side, the leverage, the stop's ATR multiple, the take-profit multiple and the longest hold, all from the policy's sets; it never gives a price or a quantity. The stop distance is the multiple × the 15-minute ATR(14) built from the final 1m candles, the stop rounded to the tick away from the entry, the take-profit to the tick toward it. The quantity is the largest step that keeps: the loss at the stop with both fees and the slippage at most 0.25% of equity; the notional at most leverage × equity; the margin and both fees within the free wallet. The stop must lie within the first third of the distance from the entry to the liquidation price, or the entry is refused.
- **The engine.** The execution worker runs the futures engine instead of the spot one, under the same lease and fencing: a fenced step every second that, in order, applies the owner's requests, records and posts funding, protects the open position (liquidation on the mark; then the stop on the bid for a long and the ask for a short; then the take-profit; then the time exit), values the account and checks the latches, moves the run's state, and answers the trader's decisions. Protection runs whatever the agents, the run state or the owner's pause; it needs only the engine and fresh data (rule 11).
- **Decisions.** The trader's finish writes one `futures_decisions` row (the action, its choices, the position it saw and the hash of its context) in the transaction that ends its workflow; the engine answers each once in `futures_decision_answers` (filled, applied, held or refused, with codes). An entry is refused unless the run is `RUNNING`, the account is flat, the decision is at most 5 minutes old and saw the account as it is, and the day's limits allow it (24 entries a UTC day, the cooldowns). An exit or a tighter stop is taken in any run state while the position is open.
- **The trader.** Generation 7: `FUTURES_DECISION_V1` (COORDINATOR), step schema v6 whose finish is the futures decision, tools `get_snapshot`, `query_futures_candles` and `read_headlines`, at most 3 model steps and a 3-minute deadline, prompt `m11/trader.v1.md`. The scheduler starts one at each finished 15-minute bar, after a fill, and on a move of more than 1.5 × ATR15 within 5 minutes; at most one open, at most 120 a UTC day, only while the run is `RUNNING` or a position is open. The spot work (briefs, investigations, price moves, coin notes, spot decisions) starts only while a spot run is live, which on the clean volume never is. The agents' check runs one futures decision on a fixed context instead of the brief and the spot decision.
- **The market worker** runs only the perpetual's feed; the spot streams and the coin scan stop.
